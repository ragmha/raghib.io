# Examples from Raghib's writing

These examples show different modes, not one structure to copy.

## Working mode for the current archive

- **TIL:** No post is currently a standalone TIL. Keep this as an available
  format for one bounded discovery; do not shorten an essay just to fill it.
- **Blog posts:** `decide-cheaply-generate-carefully.mdx`,
  `keep-shipping-budget-limits-part-1.mdx`,
  `not-every-action-item-is-mine.mdx`, and `rfp-without-spreadsheets.mdx`.
- **Tutorials and references:** `build-your-agent-harness.mdx` and
  `leaving-vercel-dns-lessons.mdx`.

## TIL: one useful distinction

> An available model is not necessarily a funded model. Giving someone
> permission to use a cheaper model does not make it free, and it does not
> override an exhausted user budget.

— `src/content/writing/keep-shipping-budget-limits-part-1.mdx`

The discovery is narrow and practical. A TIL can explain the context and
consequence, then stop.

## Blog post: follow a question

> The spreadsheet isn't the real problem. A buyer can't tell whether a cell that
> says "Yes" is a current fact, a policy intention, or a copy of something that
> was true two years ago.

— `src/content/writing/rfp-without-spreadsheets.mdx`

The post starts from an observation, reframes the issue, and follows the
question into an experiment. The author's freewriting can retain its
thinking-aloud quality while the finished piece makes each step in the reasoning
easy to follow.

## Tutorial: explain the mechanism

> Whatever a model sends is untrusted input until the harness proves otherwise.

— `src/content/writing/build-your-agent-harness.mdx`

The tutorial connects a rule to the implementation that enforces it. Keep
prerequisites, steps, expected results, and limits visible; do not turn the
guide into an essay.
